Post-booking chat · booking to installation · 13–30 Aug vs 1–20 Sep 2026

Bookings grew 45% a day. The share that reached a live connection fell.

What booked customers ask while they wait — and where the journey now breaks. The slot-breach crisis of the last report has eased. Two new problems took its place.

Bookings  4,376  /  7,045 Install rate  41.2%  /  36.0% Messages  220,435 Install-queue chatters  5,105  /  7,253

We are winning many more bookings and converting a smaller share of them — and the bottleneck is a single step.

Bookings per day went from 243 to 352. The share that ends in a live connection fell from 41.2% to 36.0%. Almost the entire loss sits in one place: slot confirmed → technician assigned, which now sheds 38.6% of all bookings, up from 32.9%. Everything before that step runs at 99%+.

September also switched the booking flow: the J4 variants went from almost nothing to 39% of bookings at a ₹10 fee. That is not the cause. Like-for-like on bookings with equal runway, J4 converts +4.9pp better than the variants it replaced; every flow got worse within itself, which is what a capacity ceiling looks like.

The good news is real. The slot-breach population — the angriest group in the last report — shrank 31% per day, and installs now complete faster (25.1h to 24.7h median). The bad news is that two quieter problems grew in its place: cancel-and-refund chatter, and a growing population who never talk to us at all.

Scope & a data breakThe August window starts 13 Aug, not 10. This is forced by the data.

Why August starts on the 13th

The install-journey states did not exist before 13 August 2026

On 12 August the chat recorded 9 distinct USER_STATE values. On 13 August it recorded 23. Every booking-to-install state — slot confirmed, technician assigned, slot breached, address pending — has zero rows before that date, and the coarse ACTIVE state was retired the same week in favour of ACTIVE_PRE_EXPIRY. This was a state-vocabulary release, not a change in customer behaviour.

Including 10–12 August would therefore add three days of structural zeros and understate every August install figure. The install-side window in this report is 13–30 August (18 days) against 1–20 September (20 days), and every comparison is expressed per day or per customer so the unequal lengths do not distort it.

One more vocabulary break to know about: INSTALLATION_COMPLETED stopped being written on 27 August. The “just installed” tier below is affected by that and should not be read as a trend.

These figures will not match the ops “Booking To Install” sheet exactly

That sheet reports 7,953 bookings and 32.3% install for 1–20 September against 7,045 and 36.0% here. The gap is definitional: this report cohorts on a booking_confirmed event for a valid 10-digit mobile in the raw event log, while the sheet is built from its own pipeline with a partner-exit filter applied. The direction and the shape of the funnel agree — install rate down, assignment the bottleneck — and that is what to rely on. Use the ops sheet for absolute booking counts; use this for the chat-linked analysis.

September installs are right-censored

A customer who booked on 20 September has had a day to be installed. The median install takes 24.7 hours and 48.0% complete inside 24 hours, so the censoring is small — but September’s 36.0% is a slight floor, not a ceiling. August, with a month of runway, is the more complete read.

The variants are also price tiers. Median booking fee is ₹10 on J4N and J4R, ₹25 on the I family, and ₹45 on J3. September therefore moved a large share of bookings onto a much lower commitment at the point of booking.

The trap: J4 bookings are younger, not worse

A naive variant comparison gets this backwards

Compared head-on across all of September, J4 looks worse — 44.3% installed against 50.2% for I3 and J3. But J4 ramped late: its median booking lands on day 16 of the month against day 6–9 for the incumbents, and 25.1% of J4R bookings are still pending against 1.8% of J3. It is censoring, not performance.

Restricting to bookings made 1–10 September, so every variant has at least ten days of runway, reverses the finding: J4 installs at 54.1% against 49.2% for the incumbents — +4.9pp in J4’s favour, on n=377 and n=1,434.

So what did cause the decline?

Every variant that existed in both windows got worse within itself. On the variants present in both, holding the mix constant at August’s still gives 51.9% against August’s 62.5% — a -10.6pp within-variant fall, against only -1.5pp from the change in mix.

That is the same conclusion the funnel reached from the other direction: this is a capacity problem at the assignment step, hitting every flow at once as volume rose +45% per day. Changing the booking flow did not cause it, and J4 is mildly helping.

A third of bookings carry no variant at all — and they are the unserviceable ones

3,789 bookings across both windows have no GROUP_NAME. They are not a random slice: 96.9% of them cancel and 74.3% are cancelled by Wiom as unserviceable. Only 40.7% ever reach a technician assignment and 8.5% install.

They pay the fee (99.8% do), give an address, confirm a slot — and then get told no. This group alone is 31.4% of September bookings and is the single largest drag on the headline install rate. Excluding it, the attributed cohort installs at 62.5% in August and 46.9% in September.

Why the variant is blank is unconfirmed

The most likely reading is that these bookings are cancelled upstream before a variant is ever assigned, which would make the blank a symptom of unserviceability rather than a cause of anything. Worth confirming with the booking-service owners before acting on it.

Slot breachThe promised slot passed without a technician.

The angriest tier — but it is shrinking

When a promised slot is missed, frustration roughly triples: 30.2% against 10.4% in the ordinary queue. The important change is the size of the group. 514 messages a day in August, 352 in September — down 31%.

These customers barely use the suggestion chips — only 13.2% of their messages are taps, against 45.3% in the calm queue. When people are angry they stop tapping and start typing, which is exactly when the canned answers stop being able to help.

This is self-selection, not proof that chat causes installs

Customers who are engaged enough to ask questions are also engaged enough to be home for a technician. What is safe to say is narrower: the non-contacting cohort is growing and converts worst, so it is the right group to go looking at.

What to fixOrdered by size of the prize.

Where the installs are actually being lost

01

Fix partner assignment, not the app

38.6% of all bookings lost at one step — 2,746 customers in September

Every app-side step runs at 99%+. Every field step after arrival runs above 93%. The single deteriorating transition is getting a partner to accept the job, and it is where nearly two thirds of all lost bookings go. No amount of chat or UI work moves this number.

Change: Treat assignment latency as the primary install KPI, and instrument why a confirmed slot fails to convert into an assigned technician — capacity, distance, or partner refusal.
02

Stop taking bookings we cannot serve

3,485 of 4,171 September cancellations were Wiom cancelling as unserviceable

These customers pay a booking fee, wait, and are then told no. It is the largest single outcome in the whole dataset and it is growing faster than bookings. The same group shows up from the other side as the 3,789 bookings with no variant set, 96.9% of which cancel — strip them out and the install rate is 46.9%, not 36.0%.

Change: Move the serviceability verdict before payment. Every one of these is a refund, a support contact, and a customer who will not come back.
03

Watch the price signal

Price as a cancel reason: 6.8% → 14.2% of customer cancels

Delay-driven cancels are falling as installs speed up. Price-driven cancels are rising to fill the gap, and the same shift shows in pre-booking chat, where cost is the top topic.

Change: Confirm against the PAYG pricing rollout before reading this as a pricing problem — but instrument it now, because it is the only cancel reason moving against us.
04

Give the slot-breach group a non-typing path

30.2% frustrated · only 13.2% chip use

The tier is shrinking, which is the win. But those still in it have effectively no chips — they type, and typing is where the dead ends and the anger are.

Change: Ship breach-specific chips: a real new date, a callback, or a cancel with an immediate refund. Stop offering the same status chip that just told them bad news.

Review notes & limits

August is 13–30, not 10–30. The install states did not exist before 13 August. Every install-side figure uses the shorter window and is normalised per day or per customer.
data break
No per-state denominator. Frustration and cancel rates are of customers who chatted, not of everyone in that state. There is still no outbound-workflow join to give a per-stage population.
structural
September installs are right-censored, though only mildly — 48.0% of installs complete within 24 hours.
timing
INSTALLATION_COMPLETED was retired on 27 August, so the “just installed” tier is not comparable across windows.
data break
BOOKING_LOGS is heavily duplicated by replication — millions of rows against thousands of customers. Every funnel figure counts distinct mobiles, never rows.
method
The warehouse journey table is still stale. FCT_BOOKING_TO_INSTALL_JOURNEY holds 98 rows from May 2026, unchanged since the July report, so the funnel is rebuilt from the raw event log.
source
Variant comparisons are censoring-sensitive. J4 ramped late in September, so any all-month variant comparison understates it. The like-for-like cut (booked 1–10 Sep) is the one to quote.
timing
Variant is attached by matching the booking record on mobile and taking the nearest booking in time. Roughly a third of confirmed bookings have no GROUP_NAME at all.
method
Install-by-chat-volume is correlational. See the caveat in that section; do not read it as “chatting causes installs”.
inference
Sentiment is a model label, and only the customer half of each conversation is stored. This measures what people said, not whether they were helped.
measurement
Glossary

Words used here

Booked
A booking_confirmed event for a valid mobile inside the window. 4,376 in 13–30 Aug, 7,045 in 1–20 Sep.
Install queue
Customers in a genuine booking-to-install state, excluding breached slots and excluding the existing customer base.
Slot breached
CX_CONFIRMED_BUT_SLOT_BREACHED and ROHIT_ASSIGNED_BUT_SLOT_BREACHED — a promised slot passed without a technician.
Existing base
Active, expired, removed and pickup states — customers with a connection, not a booking. Excluded from every install figure.
Booking variant
GROUP_NAME on the booking record — I, I1, I3, J3, J4N, J4R and a long tail. Also a price tier: ₹10 on J4, ₹25 on the I family, ₹45 on J3. Unrelated to the j2_ chat page names.
Frustrated
Share of messages the production model tagged FRUSTRATED or ANGRY.
Chip
A suggested reply. Detected as identical message text sent by three or more different customers.
Contact rate
Share of booked customers who appear anywhere in post-booking chat. A floor, since customers who chat after the window are not counted.
